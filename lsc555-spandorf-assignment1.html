San Francisco State University Library Website

San Francisco State University | J. Paul Leonard Library System

Context

The J. Paul Leonard Library serves San Francisco State University’s (SFSU) students, faculty, and staff. It is the academic library on campus that has been serving the community since 1954 and was renovated in 2012.
The mission of the library is to provide equitable access to information resources and maintain a welcoming space that allows the SFSU community to pursue their goals.

Users

Their primary goal is to serve the SFSU community, including students, faculty, and staff; they are also open to members of the general public, offering limited resources.

Content

The J Paul Leonard Library is a full-service academic library, with access to about 4 million items across its physical and digital spaces.

According to the 2025 Impact Report:
6,697 students taught by library faculty
459,862 searches conducted on OneSearch
9,843 reference questions answered
6,598 Interlibrary Loan requests delivered

SFSU community users (students, faculty, staff) can:

  • Use the library spaces
  • Use their SFSU ID to borrow physical materials
  • Get help with research
  • Meet with librarians
  • Use computers and printers
  • Browse and read non-circulating physical collections
  • Get access to course reserves
  • Use the interlibrary loan service
  • Use electronic resources inside and outside of the library using their catalog OneSearch, which includes their subscribed journals, ebooks, articles, newspapers, streaming video, data, and digital images.

The library uses OneSearch as its catalog system for users to search their collection. In their catalog, you can find information on specific resources; users can find the author, titles, and publication information, along with how to gain access to those resources if it is possible.

Observations and Unique Features

The homepage puts their catalog system, OneSearch, in the front and center of their library homepage. There, users can easily gain access to many of the library’s resources and materials.
They have an interesting “I Want To…” approach. Below the catalog, they have a section that prompts the user to find what they are looking for by reading “I Want To,” then selecting the most commonly looked-for items on the website, like “Check My Account” and “Reserve a Room.” This is a very user-friendly design.
They make it very easy to find multiple ways to “Ask a Librarian.” They have a chat option that is 24/7 and human rather than a bot, as well as email a librarian, drop-in help, and appointments with a librarian.

Pros and Cons of the System

Pros:

  • OneSearch is easy to find
  • Librarian support integrated into the website
  • Integration with other systems (Canvas, SFSU mobile app, and other California State University library systems)
  • Separate student and faculty information pages make sure all users are being addressed
  • The website has multiple purposes: discovery, research help, events, services, and access to the collections

Cons:

  • There is too much information packed into the website
  • Information is spread across multiple pages, making some things difficult to find
  • Some terminology and features may be hard to understand and learn, like advanced searching filters and databases

Suggestions for Improvement

I don’t find the SFSU J. Paul Leonard Library system and website difficult to navigate because it is similar to other academic library websites and systems that I have used. But for new users and those unfamiliar with library systems such as this, I think it would be helpful if the homepage were simplified, there was guidance on how to use OneSearch, and if they made library terminology more user-friendly.
The SFSU library system has a lot of information and integrated services, but overall I think that first-time users would need more guidance on how to use the catalog and systems offered by the library to efficiently navigate the system and gain access to resources.
