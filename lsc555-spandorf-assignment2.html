Strengths and Weaknesses

Strengths

1. Consistent and familar interface:

JSTOR uses a familiar database layout, making the site relatively easy to inticipate and navigate. Also the search bar is consistently located at the top, and filters are in predictable locations. The design is consistent with JSTOR's other databases and CUA's other digitized collections, creating a familiar experience for users.

2. Clear identification of archival materials:

Newspapers follow a standardized format for identifying issues, including dates, volume numbers, and issue numbers, which makes it easy to find an edition when you have those specific details. The website clearly displays the number of search results and provides links back to the full collection and other CUA digitized collections.

Weaknesses

1.Limited and inconsistent searching:

Searching within individual newspaper documents usually causes inconclusive or inconsistent results. It does not always make it easy to determine why a search has no results, like whether the search terms were incorrect or the material just does not exist, this makes it hard for teachers and students to find certain sources related to broader topics.

2. Difficult navigation between individual newspapers and search results:

Users can only view one newspaper at a time, making it difficult to compare issues or browse multiple newspaper issues. This can interrupt the research process, especially for students who are be looking at a topic and want to go between several issues to find relevant material.


Searching within a documents gives results, but when clicking through those results it is unclear where the indicated words are, and it works inconsistently within different documents.

Heuristic Review

The Tower Archive, digitized on JSTOR, prioritizes the preservation of archival documents over the user's ability to efficiently locate content for research.

  • The Website loads in an appropriate amount of time
  • JSTOR looks and works like other databases, with the filters in a reliable location, and having the search bar on top.
  • They use standard dating for the newspapers: date, issue, and volume number.
  • You can only view one newspaper at a time, it is not easy to go back to your previous search to view another newspaper.
  • In terms of error prevention the website makes it clear when there are no results in a search, but not when it is because of mistake with your search.
  • Underneath the number of results there is links to take you back to the full collection and all of CUA's digitized collections, so gettting back to the beginning of the collection is easy, though going back to a previous search can only be acheived with the back arrow button.
  • The website is in line with JSTOR's design and colors, along with being consistent with CUA's other digitized collections.
  • The search bar is easy to find, always on the top, though searching within a document you have seleceted usually yields inconclusive and inconsistent results.

Part 2: Mini-Usability Test

Comparison of Our Results

I was lucky enough to do a mini-usability test with someone who is familiar with computers and software systems, though they did not have much experience with database and catalog searching. They were able to recognize when the website was not intuitive, though it took some practice for them to get used to searching and finding resources.

I noticed that they did not search by date or newspaper volume. Instead, they would click into a collection, like The Tower 1922-1949, and randomly click on newspaper issues within that collection. While I started by looking for specific dates using the search bar, which I found effective when looking for something specific. Once in a document, they initially tried searching within it, but became frustrated when it was difficult to use and find results, and eventually gave up. I had a similar experience when using the search feature within PDFs. It would show that results came up, but when I clicked through them, it was unclear where the indicated words were.

Another thing I found interesting was that they were not familiar with catalog searching and assumed the database search worked like Google. When that search had no results, they gave up on using it and instead tried finding newspapers within the collections by scrolling through and looking for dates that seemed promising. When I was searching, I did not spend much time clicking through the collections and instead relied on the search bar and the results that came up. Eventually, my test subject figured out how to filter by date range, which ultimately helped them.

Overall, what I found is that someone with a basic familiarity with browsing collections can use the website fairly easily. However, someone searching for specific resources for research needs a better understanding of catalog searching and filters. I think the website could be improved by providing clearer search instructions, making the search within individual newspapers more reliable, and making filters like date range easier to find. This would make it easier for undergraduate students and teachers to find more relevant sources, especially for those who do not have much experience with archival databases.
